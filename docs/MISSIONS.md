# NBPT Living — The Five Missions

*Design doc, June 2026. Mission 1 exists; 2–5 are designed here. Every mission owns a
different part of the real map, teaches one new mechanic, and pays out a reward the
player will need on the night of the Great Fire. Writing follows the Jack rules: short
lines, real words, jokes kept, spooky welcome.*

---

## How the spine works

Five missions, one town. Each mission ends with **a tool and a friend**. The tools make
the map bigger (boat, spyglass, cart, whistle). The friends come back — all of them —
on the climax night. You don't grind for the finale; you *meet* it.

| # | Mission | Area | Tool earned | Friend earned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Wharf Rats** *(live)* | Downtown + the tunnels | Smugglers' map (🧩 corners), bike, lantern | Gram, the Editor |
| 2 | **The Keeper's Light** | Plum Island | The Keeper's Spyglass | The Keeper |
| 3 | **Once Known** | Old Hill Burying Ground + Bartlet Mall | The Listening Cart + Sexton's key | The Guide |
| 4 | **The Revenue Cutter** | The harbor, the river, Joppa flats | **The dory** (boat travel, permanent) | The Chief |
| 5 | **Silver in the Hill** | Scotland Road / Common Pasture | The Silver Whistle (calls Clipper — and later, help) | The Farmer |

## Mission 2 — THE KEEPER'S LIGHT
**Plum Island · the lighthouse that walks · a storm, a ghost, a ship to save**

**Hook.** Your library card gets you into the back room (Gram's card never could).
Inside: the keeper's logbook. Last entry, 1839: *"The light walked again last night."*

**Beats.**
1. **The Keeper.** At Plum Island Light you meet the keeper — last of the Lowell line,
   first family of the light since Washington appointed them. The sandbar has moved
   again. The channel is wrong. *"Lighthouses don't walk, kid." — "Ours does."*
2. **Walk the lighthouse.** Log rollers, real history, ridiculous and true: place the
   rollers, lean on the tower, and push the whole lighthouse down the dunes a few feet
   at a time while Clipper barks at it. The town's strangest errand, played straight.
3. **The storm.** Sky drops, rain comes sideways (winter-snow tech, re-aimed), the
   vignette closes in. Climb the tower. Light the lamp. Keep it lit — wind gusts try
   to take it, you feed the wick between gusts.
4. **The false light.** Through the rain: a *second* light, low on the dunes, where no
   light should be. Mooncusser. Cross the dunes in the storm, douse the false lantern —
   and for half a second there's a figure beyond it, old coat, no face you can make
   out. When you get there: footprints to the waterline. Then nothing. *(Harry is
   never confirmed. Ever.)*
5. **The ship.** A brig is in the channel, storm-blind. With the false light out and
   the true light burning, she threads the bar and makes the harbor. The keeper says
   her name quietly: *Pocahontas.* "That name's owed one."

**Mechanic taught:** aiming and tending light; reading the channel.
**Reward:** **The Keeper's Spyglass** — hold to look far (camera zoom tool, works
forever). *On fire night: you're the one who spots the glow first.*

## Mission 3 — ONCE KNOWN
**Old Hill Burying Ground + Bartlet Mall · the gentlest, truest mystery in the game**

**Hook.** The tombstone guide at the gate of Old Hill (by the Pocahontas monument)
has a problem worthy of a detective: *graves with no names.* Eighteen of the town's
earliest Black residents rest here, unmarked for three hundred years. The town wants
to fix it. The radar cart is broken. The guide needs sharp eyes and good ears.

**Beats.**
1. **The Listening Cart.** Fix it with the Editor's help (morgue callback — the old
   burial ledgers). It's a push-cart that *hums* over what the eye can't see.
2. **Listen.** Dusk in the burying ground, fireflies, the lantern from the tunnels on
   the cart's hook. Push slow. Where the hum rises, plant a flag. Audio-led search —
   no markers, no arrows, just your ears. (Jack rule: the spooky lives in the quiet.)
3. **The stories.** Each flag becomes a card — a name, a trade, a true detail from the
   ledgers. Short, warm, specific. The guide reads them aloud. Clipper sits for every
   single one, which everyone agrees is correct behavior.
4. **The Dexter corner.** Passing the famous grave, the cart hums where it shouldn't.
   Buried: nothing but a small wooden punctuation page. *"He's still salting it,"*
   the guide says, and refuses to explain.
5. **The ceremony.** Granite "Once Known" markers go in. The whole town walks up the
   hill. The Revere bell tolls once per name, across the water, from Old South.

**Mechanic taught:** sound-led discovery (the cart stays usable — it hums near buried
curiosities all over town, a hook for future secrets).
**Reward:** the cart, plus **the Sexton's key** — it opens doors under churches.
*On fire night: you'll need a door under a church.*

## Mission 4 — THE REVENUE CUTTER
**The harbor, the river, Joppa flats · earn the boat, learn the water**

**Hook.** Yankee Homecoming. The Coast Guard station throws its doors open, and the
Chief — Coast Guard City, birthplace of the fleet, 1791, she'll tell you twice —
has decided this town needs a junior cutter. One kid. One dory. One week.

**Beats.**
1. **Harbor chores.** Earn the oars in the basin: run lines, fetch the loose buoys,
   don't hit the docks (the docks file reports).
2. **The channel.** First real run: the river mouth. The Chief teaches the 1873 trick —
   *line up the two range lights and the channel is yours. Lights apart, sandbar.*
   The real towers, the real geometry, now a skill in your hands.
3. **The tide race.** A clammer is stuck on Joppa flats, tide coming in, greenheads
   committed. Race out, read the channel, mind the flats, tow him home. He pays in
   clams and excellent complaining.
4. **The false light, again.** Boats keep wandering off-channel at dusk. Someone's
   flashing a light from shore. You catch them: a bored teenager with a flashlight.
   *"It's always a bored teenager,"* sighs the Chief. (1679 Morse case: closed the
   same way.)
5. **Launch day.** The parade: you pilot the dory past the station, the horn answers,
   the Chief salutes, Clipper stands at the bow like a figurehead the whole way.

**Mechanic taught:** **boating** — the big one. The dory is yours forever.
**Reward:** the whole water half of the map — the islands, the far marsh, the serpent's
channel, up the river under the bridges. *On fire night: someone will need to leave
downtown by water.*

## Mission 5 — SILVER IN THE HILL
**Scotland Road / Common Pasture · a real silver rush, a dark mine, a cart with no brakes**

**Hook.** A farm kid at the Sunday market sells carrots off a card table — and one has
glitter on it. Real glitter. The Editor's morgue has the photo: *1874, the silver
parade, Scotland Road.* The mine was capped a century ago. The carrots don't care.

**Beats.**
1. **The trail.** Follow farmer Adams' old field notes (his great-great-grandkid sells
   the carrots) across the Common Pasture to a timber-capped shaft in the hillside.
2. **The mine.** Second dungeon (tunnel tech, new dressing): timbered drifts, dripping
   dark, walls that *glitter* in the lantern light. The tunnel mice have cousins down
   here. The cousins have wings. Clipper has opinions.
3. **THE CART.** Rails still run the main drift. The brakes do not. The set piece of
   the whole game: a banking, plunging mine-cart run through the glitter-dark, Clipper's
   ears flat the entire time. (Pure rollercoaster. Kids replay this forever.)
4. **The cache.** At the end of the vein: the miners' strongbox. Inside, no bars — a
   spoon mold stamped TOWLE and one honest nugget. The vein died; the silver went to
   the spoon factory; the spoons went to every wedding in America. The town WAS the
   mine. (Clipper finds the nugget. Naturally.)
5. **The parade.** Surface to the reenactment downtown — and a silversmith's descendant
   casts your nugget, while you watch, into a small bright whistle.

**Mechanic taught:** the cart ride (rail-riding tech — reusable for any future track).
**Reward:** **The Silver Whistle** — blow it and Clipper comes from anywhere on the
map. *On fire night, it turns out to call more than Clipper.*

## Where it all points: THE LONGEST NIGHT (the climax, sketched)

May 31st. The town goes to bed. You can't sleep — and through the **spyglass**, low
over downtown: a glow that shouldn't be there. A stable, near Inn Street. 1811 is
happening again, and you're the only one who knows the town has doors.

Everything comes home: ring Old South with the **Sexton's key**, spot the spread with
the **spyglass**, run the **bucket line** with Gram and the Editor and the Chief and
the Keeper and the guide and the farm kid, route the trapped through the **tunnels**
(the map you assembled), ferry families off the waterfront in the **dory**, and when
someone's lost in the smoke — the **whistle**. Every friend made, every tool earned,
one night, one town saved. Then brick, and morning, and the downtown that matches.

boats (dory), history cards (stories), synth audio (hums, horns, bells).*
