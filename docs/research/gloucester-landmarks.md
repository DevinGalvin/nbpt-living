# Gloucester + Rockport, MA — research dossier (July 13, 2026)

Web-research pass for the Gloucester town build (Rockport shares the map frame).
Coords are WGS84 lat,lon — snap to world.json features before shipping.
Bbox honored: lat 42.555–42.695, lon -70.725–-70.560. Confidence: H = two
independent sources or an exact OSM/Wikipedia node; M = single source or
derived from adjacent verified features; L = plausible but unverified.

Coordinate sources: Nominatim (OSM), Overpass (OSM nodes/ways), Wikipedia
coordinates API, and the Babson Boulder Trail GPX (adeadguy.com) — the boulder
positions are CROSS-CONFIRMED by two independent datasets (OSM historic=boulder
nodes vs. trail GPX waypoints, all within ~10 m).

Identity facts up front: settled 1623 — **America's Oldest Seaport** (the
official brand); locals genuinely say **"Fishtown"** (the July 3 parade is
literally the *Fishtown Horribles Parade*, fishtownhorribles.org); the high
school teams are the **GHS Fishermen** (gloucesterfishermenathletics.com).
Everything orbits the fishing fleet, the Fiesta, and the granite.

## B — The specific questions

### B1. SPAWN HEART: the Man at the Wheel, Stacy Boulevard (42.61014,-70.67036)

Pick: **Man at the Wheel**, and it isn't close. Evidence:
- It is "the symbol of the city... one of New England's most recognized
  landmarks," viewed by thousands annually (Wikipedia "Gloucester Fisherman's
  Memorial"; discovergloucester.com; addisonchoate.com). It's the one thing
  every Gloucester kid — and every visitor — is taken to see first.
- It satisfies "downtown + walkable to more sights" *better than Main Street
  itself*: from the statue you can SEE Ten Pound Island, Rocky Neck, Eastern
  Point, and the harbor; the Fishermen's Wives Memorial (450 m), the Cut
  drawbridge (350 m), the Coast Guard Aviation Monument (160 m), Pavilion Beach
  + greasy pole (400 m), and St. Peter's Square (500 m) are all on or just off
  the same esplanade; Main Street is an 800 m walk.
- St. Peter's Square is the runner-up (Fiesta heart, working waterfront) but
  outside Fiesta week it's a plain paved square; Main St has no single icon.
- Bonus symmetry: the spawn faces Ten Pound Island — the flight-launch site.

### B2. AVIATION: yes — Ten Pound Island, and it's even better than Salem's story

**Verified.** Coast Guard Aviation Station Ten Pound Island, Gloucester Harbor:
- **1925**: the first *successful* USCG air unit stood up here with one Vought
  UO-1 seaplane borrowed from the Navy — "Base 7," an Army-surplus **canvas
  hangar** and a crew of two, flying Prohibition rum-runner patrols
  (Wikipedia "Coast Guard Aviation Station Ten Pound Island"; history.uscg.mil).
- **20 June 1925**: first-ever use of an aircraft to chase a rum-runner; first
  aviation-assisted capture four days later (24 June 1925).
- **1926**: made permanent — the granite island was blasted level, a concrete
  ramp and steel hangar built (cgaviationhistory.org, "1926: The First
  Permanent Coast Guard Air Stations").
- Aircraft flown: Vought UO-1/UO-4, Loening OL-5, Grumman JF-2. 212 assists to
  mariners in the first four years.
- **1935**: closed, replaced by **CG Air Station Salem** — i.e., the game's
  Salem flight site is this station's direct successor. Launching Gloucester's
  scenic seaplane flight from Ten Pound Island makes the two towns' aviation
  stories one continuous arc, and the **Coast Guard Aviation Monument** on
  Stacy Blvd (42.61015,-70.66845, OSM-verified inscription) sits right at the
  proposed spawn to tell it.
- Other Gloucester aviation: nothing comparable — Ten Pound Island IS the
  story (Cape Ann's only airfield history otherwise is trivial). Bonus island
  lore for captions: Winslow Homer boarded with the lighthouse keeper in 1880.

### B3. WINTER

- **Skating (the real named pond): Niles Pond**, Eastern Point —
  42.59057,-70.65469 (H, OSM). Big, shallow ("no worries about anyone
  drowning" — northshorekid.com), and famous for kids' **ice sailing** when
  wind and black ice align (Good Morning Gloucester, Jan 2019 "Niles Pond East
  Gloucester kids ice sailing"). Backups: Fernwood Lake, West Gloucester
  (42.61115,-70.69955, "lots of skaters" per GMG) and indoor Talbot Rink
  (42.62897,-70.67291).
- **THE sledding hill: Stage Fort Park** — the big open hill by the visitor
  center, ≈42.6060,-70.6777 (M for the exact run; park H). Northshorekid.com
  lists exactly two Gloucester sledding spots: **Stage Fort Park** and **Bass
  Rocks Golf Club** (42.60885,-70.63684, H via Photon/OSM golf_course), and
  GMG photo posts show Stage Fort "full of kids sledding" after storms. Stage
  Fort is the pick (public, central, already in the roster); Bass Rocks is the
  East Gloucester alternate.

### B4. Nicknames + mascot

- **"Fishtown"** — genuinely local, not tourist-brochure: the city's July 3
  parade is the **Fishtown Horribles Parade** (fishtownhorribles.org).
- **"America's Oldest Seaport"** — the official/marketing identity (ACHP,
  NOAA, discovergloucester.com). Both usable in-game.
- Locals pronounce it "GLOSS-tah" — fun for dialogue, don't overdo.
- **High-school mascot: the Fishermen** (Gloucester High School Fishermen,
  gloucesterfishermenathletics.com; MaxPreps). Newell Stadium caption should
  say "Fishermen."

## D — Course/set-piece ideas that fell out of the research (free to ignore)

- **Race ladder writes itself**: downtown sprint (Boulevard→Fort→St. Peter's
  Sq), a "Cut & Back" mid-race crossing both bridges, and a Rockport
  Homecoming (Good Harbor→Long Beach→Bearskin Neck→Motif #1) mirroring the
  other towns' ~0.7/2/4-mile ladder.
- **Dogtown treasure hunt** is a ready-made educational mechanic: 15+
  H-confidence carved boulders with kid-perfect one-word inscriptions.
- **The greasy pole platform is in OSM as a pier** — it can literally be a
  walkable set piece with a flag at the end.
- Seasonal hooks: Fiesta (late June, St. Peter's Sq/Pavilion Beach), Fishtown
  Horribles Parade (July 3), Turkey Day at Newell, Niles Pond ice sailing.
