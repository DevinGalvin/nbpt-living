# Ipswich, MA — hero-building architecture dossier (July 13, 2026)

Compiled for the Ipswich town build; colors marked PHOTO-VERIFIED were checked
against actual photographs viewed in-browser (Wikimedia Commons, historicipswich.net,
business/parish sites, news photos) — never prose alone. Where only one photo page
supports a color it is marked (single photo). Match buildings by the OSM name given
(quoted) or by address — Ipswich OSM names its historic houses in a distinctive
lowercase style: `the John Kimball house (1680)` (verified against a live Overpass
pull, 7/13/2026 — note there are TWO John Kimball houses; 77 High St = the 1680 one).
Essex entries at the end.

## A — Hero buildings (colors verified where marked)

### First Period / museum tier

1. **Whipple House** (OSM `the Captain John Whipple House (1677)`, way/213664168; museum says 1 South Green, OSM addr 75 S Main St) — First Period 1677, lengthened 1690 by a second gable bay; moved across Choate Bridge to the South Green 1927. National Historic Landmark (1960, one of the first). 2.5 storeys, very steep gable, PHOTO-VERIFIED (Commons color photo): **near-BLACK / very dark brown clapboard**, trim the same dark tone, weathered gray-brown wood-shingle roof, massive RED BRICK central chimney, diamond-pane leaded casements, tiny second-storey windows; colonial-revival herb garden + low fence in front. Footprint: long rectangle (two-cell + chimney bay). CONFIDENCE: HIGH (Commons photo + historicipswich.net/the-whipple-house-south-green/).
2. **Alexander Knight House** (reconstruction; NOT in OSM — stands on the Ipswich Museum lawn beside the Whipple House) — 2015 volunteer replica of a 1657 town-built pauper's cottage, 16 × 12 ft, one room + loft. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **unpainted silver-gray weathered pine boards** (wide, edge-lapped), steep gable with **THATCHED roof** (phragmites reed), big square board-encased wattle-and-daub chimney at one gable end, shuttered unglazed window openings, rustic paling fence. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (historicipswich.net/2024/02/17/the-sad-story-of-alexander-knight/ + Alamy 358467651).
3. **Heard House / Ipswich Museum** (OSM `Heard House`, 54 S Main St) — Federal, 1795 (museum "c.1795–1800"), built by China-trade merchant John Heard. **3 storeys** (third-floor windows shorter — two sources say 2.5 vs 3; the photo shows 3 rows), low HIP roof. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **cream / pale ivory clapboard**, white/cream trim, **DARK GREEN louvered shutters**, gray roof, 4+ tall red-brick chimneys, flat-roofed entry portico on paired white columns, granite steps with iron rails, arched-head window over the entrance, long cream 1–1.5-storey service wings stepping down the north side + barn behind; "IPSWICH museum" lawn sign. Footprint: near-square main block + wings. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (historicipswich.net/54-s-main-heard-house/ hero photo, viewed).
4. **Hart House (restaurant "1640 Hart House")** (OSM `the Hart House (1678)`, 51 Linebrook Rd) — oldest section dendro-dated c.1680 (the "1640" is branding); now a sprawling restaurant cluster. PHOTO-VERIFIED (restaurant's own site + historicipswich): **light TAN / PUTTY-BEIGE clapboard and shingle** (NOT dark brown — classic trap), white/cream trim, natural-wood doors, big red-brick central chimney (lettered), dark gray-brown roofs; tall 2.5–3-storey GAMBREL block at one end, 1.5–2-storey gable wing with gabled/shed dormers, flat-roofed 1-storey dining section with continuous windows and a roof deck with WHITE balustrade + tan umbrellas; fieldstone wall at the road. Footprint: irregular accreted L/U cluster. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (2 photo sources: 1640harthouse.com hero + historicipswich.net/hart-house-linebrook-rd/).
5. **Waldo-Caldwell House** (OSM `the John and Sarah Dillingham Caldwell house (1660)`, 33 High St) — First Period c.1660/1709, two-over-two-room central-chimney plan. 2 storeys, side gable, weathered **gray wood-shingle roof**, large central chimney. PHOTO-VERIFIED (single photo): **dark chocolate-brown clapboards**, cream/off-white door surround, gray-tan board door, no shutters, small-pane sash with reddish-brown casings, pale central chimney at the ridge, granite steps up the banked yard. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (two photos: historicipswich.net/waldo-caldwell-house-33-high-street/ + independent web photo, both viewed).
6. **John Kimball House** (OSM `the John Kimball house (1680)`, 77 High St) — First Period 1680. 2 storeys, side gable; signature features: **12-inch second-storey jetty/overhang** with early molded gutter, and a "Beverly jog" side extension with second entrance. PHOTO-VERIFIED (two photos): **barn-red / brick-red clapboards with matching red window casings**, pale cream paneled DOOR SURROUND (the one light accent), no shutters, dark gray roof; the "Beverly jog" reads as a lower gable extension on the left side; rear additions. Chimney placement not visible in photos — model central chimney only if confirmed. CONFIDENCE: HIGH for colors (historicipswich.net/john-kimball-house-77-high-st/ + independent web photo, both viewed).
7. **Capt. Matthew Perkins House** (OSM `the Captain Matthew Perkins house (1701)`, 8 East St — East St, not High, but same First Period showcase tier) — 1701–09, 2.5 storeys, steep side gable, post-medieval **overhangs on front and side**, preservation-protected wood-shingle roof and **elaborate red-brick pilastered chimney** (big, decorated stack — model it). PHOTO-VERIFIED (two photos): **white/off-white clapboards** (weathered), dark sash, natural wood door, weathered green-gray wood-shingle roof, jetty line clearly visible across the facade, massive red-brick decorated chimney at the ridge. Footprint: rectangle + rear ell. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (historicipswich.net/8-east-st-matthew-perkins-house/ + archipedianewengland.org photo, both viewed).

### Civic / downtown

8. **Ipswich Town Hall** (OSM `Ipswich Town Hall`, 25 Green St) — built 1935–37 as Ipswich High School (PWA-era; WWI memorial), Town Hall since 2000. Classical/Colonial Revival: **red brick, monumental WHITE portico of 4 colossal columns** with white entablature, white trim bands, FLAT roof with brick parapet; 3-storey center block, long lower wings, taller gym block at one end with tall arched windows. Footprint: long symmetrical bar + center pavilion, on a big lawn. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (vintage postcard on historicipswich.net/30-green-street-town-hall/ + modern photo on ipswichma.gov Facility page — appearance unchanged).
9. **Old Town Hall** (OSM: UNNAMED way/213664216 — match by addr 30 South Main St) — 1833, first Greek Revival public building in town (built as a Unitarian church; town hall 1843; later District Court; now condos — "THE DISTRICT" sign over the door). 2 storeys, front-gable pedimented TEMPLE FRONT with boxed pilasters (the original Doric columns were removed in 1876; the old belfry is also GONE — no cupola today). PHOTO-VERIFIED: **pale sage-green clapboard body, darker gray-green pilasters/trim, BLACK window sash, black double door**, granite steps, gray roof. Footprint: rectangle (~63 × 44 ft) + big 1876 rear addition. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (current real-estate photo on historicipswich.net/old-town-hall/, viewed).
10. **Ipswich Public Library** (OSM `Ipswich Public Library`, 25 N Main St) — 1869, Augustine Heard bequest; classical front-gable block. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **red brick with pale gray GRANITE QUOINS**, full pediment with modillioned cornice, gray stone entry porch on paired columns, **natural-oak double door** with fanlight + "LIBRARY" letters, round-arched white-muntin windows on the 1-storey flanking wings, dark gray roof, black lantern on a curved post, low baluster fence. Footprint: tall 2-storey center block + symmetric 1-storey wings. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (historicipswich.net/ipswich-public-library-25-north-main-street/, viewed).
11. **First Church in Ipswich** (OSM: NO name/PoW tag — unnamed building on Meeting House Green, best candidate way/213664005; addr 1 Meetinghouse Green) — TODAY'S building is the SIXTH meetinghouse, 1971, contemporary style: the beloved 1846 Gothic burned after a lightning strike June 13, 1965. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **white / very pale brick**, dominant square front TOWER with tall amber/gold vertical glass strips and CLOCK faces near the top, **white tapered spire** carrying the historic ~40 lb **gilded rooster weathervane** (on every meetinghouse here since the 1740s) + the salvaged bell; broad gray-shingle gable sanctuary with pointed amber windows; low white 2-storey parish wings. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (historicipswich.net/first-congregational-church-1-meeting-house-green/, viewed; firstchurchipswich.org/history/). DO NOT model the pre-1965 white Gothic church.
12. **Ipswich Methodist Church** (OSM: amenity=place_of_worship, NO name tag, way/213664018 — match by addr 31 N Main St, facing Meetinghouse Green) — 1859 Italianate; nave 62 × 84 ft. PHOTO-VERIFIED (single photo): **white clapboard**, tall round-arched dark stained-glass windows, bracketed cornice, front-corner SQUARE TOWER: clock stage, round louvered oculi, octagonal louvered lantern, slender spire (**1996 fiberglass replica** of the 60-ft original — a historic mariners' landmark, on the town seal). Sanctuary ceiling collapsed 2018; renovated, reopened Dec 2025. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH (historicipswich.net/methodist-church-31-north-main-street/).
13. **Ascension Memorial Church** (OSM `Ascension Memorial Church`, 31 County St) — Episcopal, James Renwick Jr., 1869–75. **WOOD Carpenter-Gothic, NOT stone** (the "Gothic stone?" guess was wrong — photo caught it). PHOTO-VERIFIED: **olive/taupe-gray body** (board-and-batten front gable + clapboard), **rich dark-brown trim** (parish's own "historically sensitive palette" description matches photo), **crimson-RED double doors** with black strap hinges in a pointed arch, round rose window in the gable, gold cross at peak, pierced-tracery pointed windows; slender square BELL TOWER at the side street corner — open pointed-arch belfry, visible bell, steep pyramidal cap (no spire); dark brown shingle roof; attached pumpkin-tan Colonial parish hall (Boone Hall, 1960) behind. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (historicipswich.net/ascension-memorial-episcopal-church/ photo, viewed + amcipswich.org prose).
14. **Central Fire Station** (OSM `Ipswich Fire Department`, 55 Central St) — 1907, built for horse-drawn apparatus, continuously staffed since. PHOTO-VERIFIED (single photo): **red brick**, 2 storeys, **three round-arched bays** with white keystoned arches (signed SQUAD / ENGINE / ENGINE), paired windows in the arch heads, white bracketed cornice, dark gray HIP roof with a large central monitor/dormer, brick chimney. Corner rectangular block. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH (historicipswich.net/the-ipswich-central-street-fire-house/).
15. **Ipswich station (MBTA)** (OSM node `Ipswich`, 4 Topsfield Rd) — there is NO depot building: the B&M depot was demolished by 1962. Model: **one bare asphalt side platform** on a single track, yellow tactile edge, short **mini-high accessible platform** at the north end, purple-and-white MBTA "IPSWICH" signs, no shelter, parking lot alongside, two storage tracks just south. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (Wikipedia "Ipswich station (MBTA)" 2017 photo).
16. **Hall-Haskell House (Visitor Center)** (OSM `Hall-Haskell House`, 36 S Main St) — 1820, the town visitor center + seasonal gallery. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **barn-red clapboards**, dark green/black paneled door + window surrounds, white sash, pale weathered wood-shingle roof, 2 storeys, side gable, plain rectangle. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH (historicipswich.net/ipswich-visitor-center/).

### Icons

17. **Clam Box** (OSM name should read `Clam Box`, 246 High St / Rt 1A) — 1935 roadside-architecture landmark by Richard Greenleaf: the building IS an open fried-clam takeout box — walls flare OUTWARD toward the top and the tall false-front "flaps" split in a **V-notch at the front center**. PHOTO-VERIFIED (own site + historicipswich): **blue-gray shingle siding** (originally silver w/ red trim — today gray), white trim edging the flap tops, big WHITE sign panels on the flaps with RED letters "SINCE 1935 CLAM BOX", vertical lightbulb ARROW marquee ("CLAM BOX" red-on-white), red menu-word fascia band ("FRIED CLAMS SHRIMP SCALLOPS CHICKEN..."), **RED-and-WHITE striped awnings** down both sides, **RED doors** with diamond-lattice windows, small gray gable canopy over the entry, gray-brown shingled 1-storey rear dining wing (a later addition), window boxes with red geraniums, flag on the roof. ~1 storey + 2-storey-tall box front. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (clamboxipswich.com photo + historicipswich.net/clam-box/ photo, both viewed).
18. **Choate Bridge** (OSM `Choate Bridge`, S Main St over the Ipswich River) — 1764, the oldest documented two-span masonry arch bridge in the US; widened 1838. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **two segmental arches of rough gray-tan granite rubble**, center pier with cutwater, solid rubble-stone parapets with flat cap, date/name stone at the SW end; downtown storefront buildings crowd right up against the east abutment (a gray clapboard block overhangs the water). CONFIDENCE: HIGH (Commons photos + HAER).
19. **The Great House at Castle Hill** (OSM `Castle Hill (Crane Estate)` area; the mansion itself `Great House`, 290 Argilla Rd; Trustees) — 1928, David Adler, 59-room Stuart/English-Renaissance manor atop the drumlin. PHOTO-VERIFIED: **rosy-buff / salmon-tan BRICK** (not deep red), **pale limestone/cast-stone quoins + window surrounds**, white 12/12 sashes, **gray-GREEN graduated slate hip roofs**, small hip dormers, multiple tall red-brick chimney stacks, white rooftop BALUSTRADE around the central hip crown, white octagonal **CUPOLA with pale-green (verdigris) dome**, carved stone entry frontispiece with broken pediment; symmetric composition — 2.5-storey center block, lower hyphens, projecting 2-storey end pavilions + a long service wing west. Gravel forecourt with ball-topped gate piers. Footprint: long H/E-plan bar (~160+ ft). CONFIDENCE: HIGH (Commons "Castle Hill on the Crane Estate, October 2021" photo, viewed).
20. **Grand Allée (Castle Hill)** — not a building: a half-mile mowed LAWN CORRIDOR rolling from the Great House down over hills to the sea, walled by dark evergreen/deciduous allée plantings, punctuated with classical statuary; the "Casino" (garden pavilion complex) sits partway down. Model as landscape + statues. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH (Commons category photos).
21. **Crane Beach bathhouse** (Crane Beach, Argilla Rd end) — modern Trustees beach pavilion(s): long 1-storey, raised on wood piers over the sand, **natural cedar-shingle walls (light honey-tan)**, dark brown asphalt gable roofs, white-trimmed doors, small gabled entry with blue "bathhouse" sign, broad wooden decks/boardwalks with wood-and-cable rails, wide stairs to the sand. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM (one clear photo).
22. **EBSCO / former Ipswich Mills + Ipswich Mills Dam** (downtown, Ipswich River at Union/Sylvania ways; EBSCO HQ 10 Estes St) — the hosiery-mill-era complex now EBSCO: **red brick industrial blocks, 2–4 storeys**, big multi-pane windows in segmental-arch openings (some arched ground-floor bays), flat roofs w/ modern HVAC, concrete riverwalk edge; the DAM is a low broad concrete/granite **weir** — smooth water curtain in two stepped sections, stone abutments, rocky tailrace, fish ladder at one end. Signature detail: the **Ipswich Riverwalk MURAL** — a 2,700 sq ft Alan Pearsall history mural (2005, EBSCO-commissioned) painted across one mill wall facing the Riverwalk; render as a decal band and locals will cheer. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH for massing/colors (historicipswich.net dam-survey photo, viewed; mural: historicipswich.net/2022/12/19/the-ipswich-riverwalk-mural/); exact storey mix varies per block — check world.json footprints per building.
23. **Zumi's Espresso + Ice Cream** (40 Market St) — downtown storefront icon. PHOTO-VERIFIED (single photo): 1-storey commercial block, **light gray paint with charcoal panel accents**, big plate-glass windows + transoms, dark paneled door, long **BLACK sign band with gold "ZUMIS" letters** + gold cone motifs, projecting white hanging sign with red coffee-cup logo, white cone sconces, flower window boxes, red café chairs on the sidewalk. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH.
24. **Ipswich Ale Brewery** (2 Brewery Pl, off Hayward St) — UNVERIFIED — no photo-bearing source obtained for the building (industrial-park utilitarian block + taproom). Do not model detail; generic light industrial box if needed.

### Farm / roadside

25. **Russell Orchards** (143 Argilla Rd, on the way to Crane Beach) — the big 1800s store barn is **NOT painted red**: PHOTO-VERIFIED from two angles — **weathered natural wood**: honey-amber bare vertical boards on the pick-your-own gable front, gray-brown cedar shingle on the store side, **DARK GREEN sliding barn doors** with a long multi-pane transom strip, white trim/corner boards, **SILVER standing-seam/corrugated metal roofs**, white picket fence, white signpost ("Russell Orchards Farm & Winery" white/green + red "PICK YOUR OWN"), old iron hay rake + picnic tables in the yard, gray outbuildings + animal barns behind. CONFIDENCE: HIGH (2 independent photos).
26. **Marini Farm stand** (259 Linebrook Rd) — UNVERIFIED — only a historic B&W photo of the old white roadside stand found; today's stand is a much larger modern farm store (plus the famous fall corn maze). Do not model colors without a current photo.
27. **Wolf Hollow** (OSM `Wolf Hollow`, 114 Essex Rd / Rt 133) — gray-wolf sanctuary; the roadside face is the icon: **tall BARN-RED vertical-board stockade fence**, black hanging sign on a red post — white "Wolf Hollow" letters + gray howling-wolf-and-moon logo, white info sign with pink letters on the fence; low sheds + chain-link enclosures behind. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM-HIGH for the entrance (photo viewed); interior buildings LOW — keep generic.
28. **Appleton Farms** (219 County Rd, Trustees; one of America's oldest continuously operating farms, 1638) — the visitor-facing BARN CLUSTER: big gambrel-roofed barns, **pale gray-white shingle walls, white trim, dark gray roofs**, gable dormers, huge sliding doors, split-rail fences (photo-verified, single event photo). The 18th-c. "Old House" mansion itself: UNVERIFIED — no usable photo obtained; do not model its colors without one. CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM (barns) / UNVERIFIED (Old House).

### Essex nods

29. **Woodman's of Essex** (OSM `Woodman's of Essex`, 119-121 Main St, Essex — "birthplace of the fried clam," 1914/1916 Chubby Woodman legend). PHOTO-VERIFIED (2 photos): **gray shingle + gray vertical-board walls, WHITE trim**, white flat entry canopy on posts along the sidewalk, red-brick planter base at the corner, stacked white rooftop SIGN panels — red "WOODMAN'S SINCE 1914", navy "FAMOUS FRIED CLAMS", red "STEAMERS LOBSTERS / LOBSTER ROLLS", "...IN THE ROUGH"; big white sign band "WELCOME TO WOODMAN'S OF ESSEX — A Yankee Tradition Since 1914" (red script); external gray stair to the 2nd-floor "Top Deck" bar; 2–2.5 storeys, gable behind a shallow front block; American flag. CONFIDENCE: HIGH.
30. **Essex Shipbuilding Museum** (66 Main St, Essex — waterfront shipyard site + store; the 1835 schoolhouse campus is nearby at 28 Main St) — PHOTO-VERIFIED (main 66 Main building): **dark weathered-brown cedar shingle**, **barn-red/maroon trim** (window casings, corner boards), gable roof, gold-letter black quarterboard sign "ESSEX SHIPBUILDING MUSEUM" + "MUSEUM STORE", rooftop deck rail behind, 1.5–2 storeys, mums/garden + rustic post sign out front; schooner hulls/ways in the yard behind (Essex launched 4,000+ vessels). CONFIDENCE: HIGH for the 66 Main building; the 28 Main schoolhouse UNVERIFIED — check before modeling.

## B — Model these first (iconic-ness × confidence)

1. **Clam Box** — the town's roadside-America icon; kids know it instantly; colors fully verified.
2. **Great House at Castle Hill** — the region's grandest silhouette; verified brick/slate/cupola palette; pairs with the Grand Allée lawn.
3. **Choate Bridge** — 1764 twin stone arches IN the downtown road grid; cheap to model, huge authenticity payoff.
4. **Whipple House** — near-black First Period showpiece on the South Green; National Historic Landmark.
5. **Heard House / Ipswich Museum** — cream Federal anchor directly across from the Whipple; the two make the museum campus read instantly.
6. **First Church in Ipswich (1971)** — the Green's landmark; unusual modern-with-rooster story locals will fact-check.
7. **Ipswich Public Library** — brick + granite quoins; distinctive and verified.
8. **Ipswich Town Hall** — big red-brick colossal-column school-turned-town-hall; easy federalHouse/brick-school hybrid.
9. **Russell Orchards barn** — beloved kid destination on the Crane Beach road; weathered-wood + green doors verified (NOT red — don't guess).
10. **Woodman's of Essex** (Essex nod) — fried-clam pilgrimage site; gray/white + red signage verified.
11. **Ascension Memorial Church** — olive-and-brown Carpenter Gothic with campanile; verified and visually unique.
12. **Old Town Hall ("The District")** — sage-green temple front on S Main; verified current scheme.
13. **Hart House** — rambling putty-tan restaurant cluster with gambrel block; verified from its own site.
14. **EBSCO mills + dam** — the downtown river wall + weir + the 2,700 sq ft Riverwalk history mural decal; medium detail is fine.
15. **John Kimball House (77 High St)** — barn-red First Period with jetty overhang; best-verified of the High St row (with Waldo-Caldwell dark brown at 33 High as its neighbor-piece).
16. **Wolf Hollow entrance fence** — one red fence + black wolf sign = instant landmark.
17. **Essex Shipbuilding Museum (66 Main)** — dark shingle + red trim, verified.
18. **Alexander Knight House** — tiny thatched build, high charm-per-polygon, sits on land you're already modeling.
19. **Zumi's** — downtown storefront kids know; verified sign scheme.
20. **Hall-Haskell House** — barn-red visitor center next to Old Town Hall; simple box.
