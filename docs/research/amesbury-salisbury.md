# Amesbury + Salisbury — landmark accuracy dossier (July 25, 2026)

Photo-verification pass over every landmark in towns #9 and #10, same discipline
as `rockport-manchester.md`: **PHOTO-VERIFIED** = I looked at a photograph of
the actual building this session and read the colors off it. **UNVERIFIED** =
deliberately NOT given bespoke detail.

Of the 64 landmarks, 44 are parks, marshes, woods, cemeteries and beaches with no
building to model — they get the world's own geometry. **20 are building-backed**,
and those are what this pass covers.

---

## Amesbury

### 1. Amesbury Town Hall — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
OSM calls it `Amesbury City Hall` (the town became a city in 1996; the building's
own sign still says TOWN HALL — keep the OSM string verbatim as the HEROES key).
Ref: Commons `Amesbury Town Hall.JPG`.
**Deep red-orange BRICK**, 2 storeys, **flat roof behind a corbelled brick
parapet** with a dentil-like course of projecting headers. The front is three
bays: **two enormous round-arched windows** flanking a **round-arched entrance**,
all with pale stone imposts. Inside each arch, white multipane sash with a
fan-tracery lunette above. A **black sign board lettered AMESBURY TOWN HALL in
gold**, with a carriage on each side, sits over the entry arch. Brick pilasters
between bays, granite sills, one brick chimney at the left end, granite steps.

### 2. Josiah Bartlett Museum — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
OSM leaves the footprint unnamed (POI node only → nameFix). Ref: Commons
`Josiah Bartlett Museum (exterior)`. 1870 Italianate:
**pale cream/putty clapboard**, 2 storeys, **low hipped roof with wide
overhanging eaves carried on paired scroll BRACKETS**, **RED window casings**
and red accents in the cornice, a **red-painted water table** at the base,
a **round-arched paired window with a red arch surround** over the entry, and a
**bracketed entry porch on square columns**. One red-brick chimney.

### 3. All Saints Anglican Cathedral — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `All Saints Church, Amesbury MA.jpg`. **Red brick Gothic Revival**:
steep gable front with a **cross finial**, a **large pointed-arch traceried
window** in pale stone surround, **three pointed-arch doors** below (the centre
taller, in a gabled stone hood), and a **square brick tower** on the left with
pale stone banding and a **stepped/crenellated parapet**. Granite steps.

### 4. Whittier Home — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
86 Friend St, a National Historic Landmark; OSM doesn't name the house at all, so
the landmark is placed from the built address layer and the footprint gets a
nameFix. Ref: Commons `John Greenleaf Whittier Home`.
**WHITE clapboard**, 2 storeys, **DARK GREEN shutters on every window**, gray
shingle gable roof carrying **two front gable dormers plus a shed dormer**,
**two red-brick chimneys**, a small **entry porch on square white columns** with
a black WHITTIER HOME sign board over it and a dark green door, a long rear ell,
and a **white picket fence** at the sidewalk.

### 5. Lowell's Boat Shop — PHOTO-VERIFIED (MEDIUM-HIGH)
1793, the oldest continuously operating boat shop in America and the birthplace
of the Amesbury dory; a National Historic Landmark. Ref: Commons
`Lowell's Boat Shop, Amesbury MA.jpg` (across the river) + HAER interiors.
A **barn-RED clapboard cluster right at the waterline** on Point Shore: a taller
2.5-storey block with a lower wing, gable roofs, and **dense regular rows of
white-trimmed multipane windows** — far more glass than a house, because the
shop floors are lit from both sides. White trim, dock and ways at the water.
(Read from a mid-river photo — massing HIGH, fine trim MEDIUM.)

### 6. The Powder House — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons HABS `Powder House, Amesbury` (Haskell, 1935). Not a brick igloo:
a **round whitewashed/parged masonry cylinder with a domed top**, standing alone
on the hilltop, no windows on the photographed face. Reads as a pale bullet.
Landmark caption corrected accordingly.

### 7. The Doughboy — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `The Doughboy, Amesbury Middle School`. A **bronze WWI infantryman
mid-stride** — helmet, pack, rifle — on a **pale granite pedestal**, set against
a **low granite wall carrying two bronze relief panels**, with a flagpole beside
it. POI monument tier (`POI_HEROES`), no footprint.

### 8. Mary Baker Eddy House / Squire Bagley homestead — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
277 Main St; OSM names neither the way nor a POI, so the landmark is placed from
the built address layer and the footprint gets a nameFix. Ref: Commons
`Mary Baker Eddy Historic House - Amesbury, Massachusetts.JPG`.
Genuinely odd for New England and worth getting right: **dusty PINK clapboard**
with **deep maroon-brown trim** on every casing and corner board, **dark green
shutters** on the front, a gray shingle **GAMBREL** roof, a red-brick chimney,
and a **maroon picket fence** at the sidewalk. The pink is the whole point.

### REAL BUT UNMAPPED — deliberately not placed
**St James Episcopal Church** (79 Main St) — photo-verified as a superb
fieldstone Gothic with a crenellated bell tower and RED doors (Commons
`St James Episcopal Church, Amesbury MA.jpg`), but OSM has neither a named way
nor the address, so there is no honest coordinate. Worth an OSM edit.
**Amesbury Sports Park** and the **Chain Bridge** — same story.

### UNVERIFIED — no bespoke model
Amesbury Public Library (searched Commons, Wikipedia and the library's own site
— no exterior photo), Old Amesbury Town Hall, the Wilman Building (Stage Two
Cinema Pub), Nicolas Costello Transportation Center, Cider Hill's Farm Store
(the Commons Unsplash shots are the corn maze and pumpkin field, not the store),
the Lower Millyard mill block, Market Street Baptist, Union Congregational,
the Captain's Well, Golgotha Boulder.

## Salisbury

### 1. Salisbury Town Hall — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
The 1834 East Parish Meeting House. Refs: Commons `Salisbury MA Town Hall.jpg`,
`Town Hall, Salisbury MA.jpg`, `East Parish Meeting House, 1834, Salisbury MA`.
**WHITE clapboard Greek Revival, gable-FRONT**: a big pediment carrying a
**round oculus window with muntins**, **modillion blocks** along the raking and
eaves cornices, **corner pilasters**, tall 12-over-12 sash with **BLACK
shutters**, an **arched fanlight window** over the entry, and a small
**pedimented entry hood on brackets** lettered TOWN HALL, over brick steps.

### 2. Chubby's Diner — TYPE-VERIFIED (MEDIUM-HIGH)
72 Main St, address-layer confirmed against the footprint (12 px apart). It is a
**Jerry O'Mahony lunch car built in 1941** — that is a documented fact about this
exact building, and it is what the model asserts. The *look* of the type is
photo-verified from Commons `Pats Diner, Salisbury MA`: a barrel-vaulted
stainless roof, white porcelain panels with **RED trim bands and posts**, and a
white roof sign board lettered in red. **This is type-verified, not
photo-verified for this building** — flagged as such deliberately.
It stopped serving in 2005, so the landmark card now describes the dining car
rather than promising a breakfast; the old caption said "Breakfast in a genuine
diner", which would have sent a kid to a closed door.

### UNVERIFIED — no bespoke model
Salisbury Public Library, Blue Ocean Music Hall (nameFixed so search finds it;
a modern hall on the sand, no free photo), Star of the Sea Chapel, the
Interpretive Center, the Camp Store, Lena's Seafood, the Maria Miles Visitors
Center.

## Lessons

1. **A town's own name for a building can disagree with OSM's.** Amesbury became
   a city in 1996 and OSM says `Amesbury City Hall`; the building says TOWN HALL
   and everyone calls it that. HEROES must key on the OSM string, but the
   landmark caption should use the human one.
2. **Check what a monument is made of before calling it brick.** The Powder House
   caption said "a little brick igloo"; the 1935 HABS photo shows a whitewashed
   parged cylinder. Cheap to check, and the caption is player-facing text.
3. **The bracket is the tell on an Italianate.** Bartlett Museum and Amesbury's
   other 1870s civic buildings read wrong without the paired eave brackets —
   they're what separates Italianate from a plain hip-roofed box, and they cost
   two boxes per bay.
4. **Stepping little boxes around a curve reads as a STAIRCASE, not an arch.**
   The Town Hall's three great arches were first built as seven boxes each
   walking the semicircle; at play distance it looked like rubble on the wall.
   New shared `roundArch()` draws a real half-circle head as a triangle fan on
   the facade plane — use it for any Romanesque or round-arched opening.
5. **When framing fights you, audit the geometry instead of the screenshot.**
   Chasing a camera angle building-by-building burned more turns than the
   modelling did. Scanning the built chunk mesh near a footprint for the hero's
   own distinguishing hex values proves the builder ran AND that its detail
   landed — every hero in this pass was confirmed that way (City Hall 3/3,
   Whittier 3/3, All Saints 2/2, Bartlett 3/3, Lowell's 3/3, Powder House 2/2,
   the Doughboy 3/3, Salisbury Town Hall 4/4, and in the second wave the Eddy
   House 3/3 and Chubby's 3/3). Screenshot once, to taste.
6. **"In the frame" is not "in the town".** The second landmark wave started from
   a POI sweep of the built world and half the best candidates — Markey's Lobster
   Pool, the Newburyport waterfront, Seabrook's schools, Merrimac's town hall —
   sit outside the municipal line the frame overruns. `landmark_candidates`
   boundary-checks named FEATURES, but a raw POI sweep does not, so the wave got
   its own point-in-boundary check against `data/<town>/raw/boundaries.json`
   (stitching the relation's outer ways into rings first — they arrive unordered
   and some reversed).
7. **Check whether the place is still open before promising it.** Chubby's closed
   in 2005; the first caption invited a kid to breakfast there.
