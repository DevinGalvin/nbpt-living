## 2. Direct answers

### 1. SPAWN HEART — Five Corners / Market Square: **42.68005, -70.83750** (H)
The intersection of North Main, South Main, Market, and Central — traditionally **Market Square**, known to everyone today as **Five Corners** ([The Local News](https://thelocalnews.news/2025/01/07/five-corners-traffic-light-at-the-center-of-state-transportation-improvement-program/), [Historic Ipswich Market Square](https://historicipswich.net/2022/11/26/market-square/)). Coords are the exact shared OSM way nodes of Market St + North Main St. Evidence it's the heart: it is a famous "courtesy intersection" with no traffic light (a decades-long town controversy — deeply local color); Zumi's is on the corner; the Choate Bridge is 70 m south; the Riverwalk mural, footbridge, and Mills Dam are a 2-minute walk. A local kid takes a visitor here first, buys a cocoa at Zumi's, walks them over the Choate Bridge, and down the Riverwalk. Drop the player mid-square facing the Choate Bridge.

### 2. AVIATION — yes, real and early: **Moulton's Farm, Essex Road (1910)**
- **Nov 27, 1910:** first test flight of the Burgess Company (Marblehead) **Model D "pusher" biplane** at Moulton's Farm on Essex Road; William M. Hilliard then flew passenger flights there; Gardiner + F. Tracy Hubbard tested three monoplanes there in 1911 ([Historic Ipswich, "Taking to the Air in Ipswich, 1910"](https://historicipswich.net/2021/04/30/burgess_flight-ipswich/)).
- Site precision: the article places it "near Northgate Road on Joseph Marshall's property or the 60-acre Brown estate (now Maplecroft Farm)". Northgate Rd meets Essex Rd at ≈ **42.6597, -70.8087** (Nominatim; field-site precision **LOW-MED** — flat farm fields just north of that junction fit the account). Brand it "**1910 Burgess Flying Field**" and launch the scenic flight there — same Burgess lineage as NBPT's Plum Island story (Burgess also flew at Plum Island in April 1910: nice cross-town Echo).
- Bonus: **Crocker Snow** of Topsfield Rd, Ipswich — Mass. Pilot License No. 5 (1927), signed by Orville Wright; his backyard strip was jokingly called "Snow International Airport" (strip location **LOW** confidence). No seaplane base or Crane Beach landing tradition surfaced — the Moulton's Farm field is the historically-justified pick.

### 4. NICKNAMES / MOTTOS locals use

- **"Clamtown"** — the local nickname; the Ipswich clam is the town's identity ([Historic Ipswich "Photos from Clamtown"](https://historicipswich.net/2025/12/02/clamtown-photos/), [GoNOMAD](https://www.gonomad.com/125005-ipswich-ma-crane-beach-famous-clams-long-history)).

- Brag locals actually make: **most First Period (1625–1725) houses in America** (~59).

## 3. Extra story hooks found along the way
- The 1687 revolt leaders: Rev. **John Wise** of Chebacco Parish (now Essex) rode to rally the Appleton brothers; jailed and fined, vindicated when Andros fell in 1689 ([Historic Ipswich](https://historicipswich.net/2018/11/23/ipswich-revolt-1687/)). Perfect history-card arc for Meeting House Green.
- **Greenheads** (biting flies) at Crane Beach in July — every North Shore kid's badge of honor; great ambient gag.
- **Bull Brook** (near Bull Brook Reservoir, 42.69378,-70.87300): one of North America's most important Paleo-Indian sites (~10,000+ yrs), found by local amateurs in a 1950s sand pit — exact site not publicly marked (LOW for placement; strong history-card material).
- The **Ipswich Mills Dam removal debate** — herring vs. history — is live civic drama for the fish-ladder card.
- Crane Estate = "the toilet king's castle": Richard T. Crane Jr.'s plumbing-fixture fortune built the Great House. Kids remember that forever.
