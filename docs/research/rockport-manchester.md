# Rockport + Manchester-by-the-Sea — landmark accuracy dossier (July 25, 2026)

Photo-verification pass for the two *neighbor villages* that ride in-frame in
existing towns: **Rockport** (in Gloucester's frame) and **Manchester-by-the-Sea**
(in Beverly's frame). Same discipline as `gloucester.md` / `ipswich.md`:
**PHOTO-VERIFIED** = I looked at a photograph of the actual building this session
and read the colors off it. **UNVERIFIED** = deliberately NOT given bespoke
detail; it gets the verified village palette and correct massing instead.

Trigger: the Motif rendered a near-black brown box ("isn't that supposed to be
red?"). It was — `#6e2f28` is too dark and desaturated once the engine's
side-shade multiplier (0.78) lands on it. Lesson at the bottom.

## Rockport (Gloucester frame)

### 1. Motif No. 1 — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Refs: Commons `Motif 1, Rockport MA.jpg`, `Motif Number 1, Rockport MA.jpg`.
The 1978 exact replica of the 1884 shack, on Bradley Wharf.

- **Two volumes, not one.** Harbor (seaward) end = a **taller gable-FRONT block**
  (~2.5 storeys, gable facing the water) with a red-brick ridge chimney, a white
  multipane window high in the gable, a small dark upper window, and **big red
  double doors** at the ground. The rest = a **lower side-gable wing** running
  inland, roof ridge clearly below the front block's.
- **Vertical board siding, medium-dark BARN RED** — reads unmistakably *red* in
  sun (~`#a8402f`), not maroon. Trim/doors the same red; window sashes white.
- **Roof: weathered silver-gray wood shingle** (~`#a8a49c`), noticeable overhang.
- **The buoy wall**: dozens of small lobster buoys hung in vertical clusters on
  the long harbor-facing wall — mostly **white/cream** with orange, red, yellow
  and a little blue. They must be big enough to read at play-camera distance.
- Sits on a **granite-block wharf** with weathered **timber fender piles** poking
  up along the water edge.

### 2. First Congregational Church — "the Old Sloop" — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
OSM name: `First United Church of Christ Congregational` (nobody calls it that).
Ref: oldsloop.org `Church-Winter-600w.jpg`.
1804 meetinghouse; steeple rebuilt 2016–17. **WHITE clapboard** throughout.
Gable-front sanctuary with a small columned porch. Tower: plain white shaft →
**square clock stage with BLACK round clock faces** (white/gold hands) →
**white balustrade** → **ROUND open columned belfry (lantern)** → **GREEN copper
dome** + finial. NOT a tapered spire — the earlier dossier said "tapered spire";
the photo settles it as a domed lantern.

### 3. First Universalist Church — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `First Universalist Church, Rockport MA.jpg`.
**WHITE clapboard, Gothic Revival.** Square white tower: pointed-arch louvered
belfry opening with colonnettes, a blind-arcade panel stage below, dentil
cornices, and a **dark tapered spire trimmed in GREEN copper** with small
pinnacles. Gable roof, dentil cornice, pointed-arch windows with white tracery.

### 4. Rockport Art Association & Museum — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: rockportartassn.org front-building photo. The **Old Tavern, 1787** sea
captain's house at 12 Main St. **WHITE clapboard**, 2.5 storeys, gray shingle
gable roof with dormers, **strong RED shutters, RED front door and RED window
boxes**, a flat white entablature entry hood lettered ROCKPORT ART ASSOCIATION.
Attached mid-1800s Maddocks barn behind.

### 5. Shalin Liu Performance Center — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
OSM spells it `Shalin Liu Perfomance Center` (missing "r" — keep verbatim).
Ref: Commons `ShalinLiuPerformanceCenter Facade.jpg`. 37 Main St, opened 2010
behind a replica 1860s Second Empire facade. 3 storeys:
cream/white storefront base with glass → **big RED-ORANGE awning with white
lettering** → middle storey in **gray-mauve clapboard with cream pilasters**,
three **red-orange French doors with white balconets**, terracotta brackets →
**gray slate MANSARD** with three arch-hooded dormers (red-orange doors +
balconets again) and a flanking window each side.

### 6. Rockport Public Library (17 School St) — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `Rockport Public Library - Rockport MA.jpg`. Unnamed footprint in
OSM — only a POI node carries the name (nameFix). **Rough-faced gray ASHLAR
GRANITE**, 1864 mill → 1904 school → library 1993. 2 storeys over a granite
base, **wide overhanging eaves on big brackets**, pedimented gable end, tall
white multipane windows with granite lintels, flagpole.

### 7. Rockport Carnegie Library (18 Jewett St) — VERIFIED BY DESCRIPTION (MEDIUM)
1907, "locally quarried **bi-color granite** with Greek Revival features … a
terrazzo floor **under the dome**" (rockportlibrary.org). A library until 1993,
now a private residence. Modeled as a small granite Greek Revival block with a
low dome — no photo obtained, so no finer detail than that.

### 8. Memorial Hall — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH), not currently a named footprint
Ref: Commons `Memorial Hall Rockport 1.jpg`. **White-painted brick**, gray
shingle roofs, a taller square entry tower with a hipped roof and a lettered
MEMORIAL HALL sign panel, a big round-arch entry with **LIGHT BLUE double
doors**, arched windows, granite base. Recorded for a future pass.

### 9. Straitsmouth Island Light — PHOTO-VERIFIED (MEDIUM)
Ref: Commons `Straitsmouth Island Lighthouse MA.JPG` (historic). Short **white**
cylindrical tower, **black lantern**, low white keeper's building at the base on
bare granite ledge.

### 10. Bearskin Neck + the harbor village — PALETTE PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Refs: Commons `Bearskin Neck in Rockport, Ma.jpg`,
`Rockport MA Harbor and Bearskin Neck DSC 0138 AD.JPG`.
The village reads **weathered gray-brown cedar SHINGLE first**, then white and
cream clapboard, then a few blues and one or two reds — with **white trim**
everywhere. Buildings are small, tight to the lane, 1.5–2.5 storeys, gable
roofs, brick chimneys, hanging painted signs. The engine was painting them from
the Newburyport *painted-clapboard* palette, which is why the village read as a
row of pale institutional blocks. Fixed with a per-town `shingleZones` rule.

### UNVERIFIED — no bespoke model (village palette + real height only)
Rockport Town Hall (34 Broadway, Colonial Revival — style confirmed, no photo),
Ellen's Harborside, Helmut's Strudel, Emerson Inn, Rockport Inn & Suites,
Rockport Elementary/Middle/High, Smith Cabin.

## Manchester-by-the-Sea (Beverly frame)

The Beverly frame reaches to lon −70.755, so Manchester's village, harbor,
Singing Beach, Tuck's Point and Masconomo Park are all in-frame — but the town
carried **zero landmarks** and zero heroes before this pass. OSM names almost
nothing here on the building ways; the names are on POI nodes (nameFix).

### 1. Manchester-by-the-Sea Town Hall — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `Manchester-by-the-Sea Town Hall.JPG`. 1868 Greek Revival temple
front: **whitewashed BRICK** (warm cream-white with red brick ghosting through),
a big triangular **pediment with a semicircular fanlight**, **four massive square
brick piers**, 2 storeys, low gable roof, **DARK GREEN double doors**, granite
steps, lantern sconces.

### 2. Manchester-by-the-Sea Public Library — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `Manchester by the Sea Public Library.jpg`. 1887 Richardsonian
Romanesque: rough-faced **variegated BROWNSTONE** (warm orange-brown with pink
and buff courses), **slate-gray steep roofs**, a **square tower** with an open
arched belfry, a **lead-gray dome and green copper finial**, a **clock face** on
the tower, and a **big round-arch entry with wooden double doors**. Granite
retaining wall at the sidewalk.

### 3. First Parish Church — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `Manchester First Parish Church.JPG`. 1809 meetinghouse.
**WHITE clapboard**, pedimented gable front, Palladian window over the center
door, **three black arched doors**, brick rear chimney. Tower: white shaft →
**gold clock face with black surround** + fanlight → **balustraded stage with
urns** → **open octagonal columned belfry** → small **green copper dome** →
weathervane.

### 4. Trask House (Manchester Historical Museum) — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Ref: Commons `Trask House Museum, Manchester by the Sea.JPG`. 1823 Federal:
**WHITE clapboard**, 2 storeys, **BLACK shutters** on every window, low hipped
roof with a **white ROOF BALUSTRADE**, white chimney, arched/pedimented door
surround over a black door, white lattice fence along the street.

### 5. Manchester harbor + Manchester Marine — PHOTO-VERIFIED (MEDIUM)
Ref: Commons `Manchester By The Sea Harbor - Manchester, Massachusetts.jpeg`.
The inner harbor's far shore is dominated by **long barn-RED boatyard sheds**
(Manchester Marine) with pale gray roofs; the rest is wooded shore, granite
riprap and a mooring field of small white boats.

### 6. Tuck's Point Rotunda + Chowder House — VERIFIED BY DESCRIPTION (MEDIUM)
Refs: manchester.ma.us, spencerpreservationgroup.com. The rotunda is a
**Victorian open pavilion by E.A.P. Newcomb**, wood frame **out over the water on
wood piles**, reached by a **wood walkway**, with a **flat-seam COPPER dome**
(replaced the original asphalt shingle) and a flagpole. The Chowder House is a
separate open-walled shelter with picnic tables on the lawn behind it. No photo
obtained → open pavilion + copper dome + pile walkway, nothing finer.

### 7. Singing Beach — PHOTO-VERIFIED (HIGH)
Refs: Commons `Singing Beach, Manchester, May 2014.JPG`, `Eaglehead …`. Sand that
squeaks underfoot, a half-mile walk from the station, with the **Eaglehead** rock
headland at the east end. Already mapped sand — it gets the beach-life pass.

### UNVERIFIED — no bespoke model
Manchester Yacht Club, The Chowderhouse footprint, Crosby's, Allen's, Captain
Dusty's (Manchester), Crowell Chapel, Cornerstone Church, Rosedale Church,
Manchester station, Manchester Post Office.

## Lessons

1. **Dark hero colors go black.** Wall colors are multiplied by
   `STYLE.building.wallDarken` (0.78) on the shaded faces and then lit. A hex
   picked to read "weathered" in a swatch (`#6e2f28`) lands near-black in game.
   Pick hero colors ~1 value-step BRIGHTER than the photograph, then check them
   in the preview — never from the swatch alone.
2. **One box is not a landmark.** The Motif was a single extruded footprint; the
   thing that makes it recognizable is the *two-volume* silhouette and the buoy
   wall. When a hero has a famous silhouette, model the massing before the trim.
3. **Neighbor villages inherit the flagship town's palette.** Rockport and
   Manchester were being painted with Newburyport's painted-clapboard house
   palette. A district palette (`shingleZones`) is the cheap general fix, and it
   fixes hundreds of buildings, not one.
4. **The name is on the node, not the way** — again. Rockport Public Library, the
   Manchester town hall, library, church and Trask House all carry their names on
   POI nodes only. `nameFixes` + `tools/patch_names.mjs`.
5. **`brickTex` bakes RED brick into the texture** (`textures.ts`: bricks ≈
   rgb(166,81,61), mortar rgb(196,186,176)). The hex passed to
   `walls(buckets[BRICK], …)` is a *tint over red*, not the building's color — a
   cream hex still renders a red brick building. That is why Manchester's
   whitewashed-brick town hall came out as a red apartment block on the first
   pass. **Granite/whitewash/stucco must use the PLAIN bucket with texScale 0**
   (as `buildCustomHouse` does). The one place the red grain *helps* is
   BROWNSTONE — Manchester's library keeps `BRICK` and passes a near-white tint
   so the texture's own rust reads as the real stone.
6. **`frontSegment`/`heroFront` fall back to the CENTROID.** If no footprint edge
   ≥ 24 px faces a road, they return the building's center — which buries a
   portico or pediment inside the building. Anything that draws a facade feature
   should go through the new `obbFront()`, which picks the OBB face best matching
   the road-facing normal and slides it onto the real wall.
7. **`gableRoof` silently flattens on concave footprints** (`OBB_ROOF_MIN_FILL`).
   An L-shaped civic footprint gets a flat roof no matter what the hero asks for
   — expected, and the right call (a draped OBB roof would span the notch), but
   worth knowing before hunting for a "missing" gable.
