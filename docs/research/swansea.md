# Swansea, MA — research dossier for Clipper Town

Researched 2026-09-15 for the `swansea` town (`towns/swansea/`, `src/towns/swansea/`).
Every card and landmark sub in the pack traces to a line here. Coordinates in the
pack are WORLD PX read off the built `world.json` (address anchors from the
MassGIS-imported `addr:*` tags, named footprints, poly interior points) — never
guessed from a map. Where OSM carries no name for a real place, the landmark sits
on its street address and says so in the notes.

Facts were gathered by web search from a cloud session (page fetches are blocked
here, so the source is the search summary of the page named — re-verify before
carving anything in stone).

## Geography

- Bristol County, on the north shore of **Mount Hope Bay**. Rivers: the **Cole**
  (through the middle, Gardner's Neck on its east bank), the **Lee** (the Somerset
  line), the **Palmer** (Barneyville, the RI line to the northwest), the Kickemuit
  (Warren, RI, to the southwest). Neighbours: Rehoboth (N), Dighton (NE), Somerset
  (E), Warren + Barrington RI (W), Seekonk (NW). Fall River across the Taunton
  River beyond Somerset.
- Five historic villages, each named for the family that held the land: the
  Gardners in **South Swansea / Gardner's Neck**, the Barneys in **Barneyville**,
  the Luthers at **Luther's Corner** (= Swansea Center), the Hales in
  **Hortonville**, the Eddys in **Swansea Village**. (Swansea Historical Society;
  Wikipedia "Hortonville, MA", "Luther's Corner", "Swansea Village Historic District".)
- Shoreline neighbourhoods of South Swansea: the Neck, **Ocean Grove** (a CDP on the
  bay near the Cole River mouth), **Touisset**. (Wikipedia "Ocean Grove, Massachusetts".)
- Route 6 = the **Grand Army of the Republic Highway** (US 6 is signed that way
  across Massachusetts); the commercial strip. I-195 crosses the north of town.
  The **Braga Bridge** (I-195, Somerset–Fall River) opened April 15, 1966, a mile
  long, ~82,000 vehicles a day, named for Charles M. Braga Jr., a Fall River sailor
  killed at Pearl Harbor. (turnto10, Wikipedia.)

## History — the spine

- **Sowams / Pokanoket.** The land was the southwestern part of Massasoit's
  Pokanoket country — Bristol, Warren, Barrington, East Providence, and parts of
  Seekonk, Swansea and Rehoboth. Massasoit's seat, Sowams, was on the Warren/
  Barrington side; Metacom (King Philip) ruled from Mount Hope in Bristol. A
  Wampanoag trail ran along today's Kickemuit Rd / School House Rd / Swansea Rd
  past a large flat "grinding" rock near the state line. (Baker, *Massasoit's Town
  Sowams in Pokanoket*, 1904 — gutenberg.org/ebooks/65284; Wikipedia "Pokanoket".)
- **John Myles, 1663.** Welsh Baptist minister (c.1621–1683), driven out of Ilston,
  Gower under Charles II; came to Rehoboth in 1663 with part of his congregation
  and organised the **First Baptist Church in Swansea** — the oldest Baptist
  congregation in Massachusetts (founded in Wales 1649). Granted land south of
  Rehoboth; the settlement was named for Swansea, Wales, the hometown; incorporated
  **1667**. (Wikipedia "John Myles (minister)", "First Baptist Church in Swansea";
  baptisthistoryhomepage.com.) The current church is an 1848 Greek Revival chapel
  at 21 Baptist Street — the congregation's fifth building. (Buildings of New
  England, 2026-04-17.)
- **"Swansea" is not about swans.** The Welsh city's name is Old Norse *Sveinsey* —
  "Sweyn's island", a Viking trading post credited to Sweyn Forkbeard (d. 1014).
  (medievalswansea.ac.uk; dictionary.com.) The town's flag carries the seal on
  blue — the seal itself is not described in the sources found.
- **King Philip's War began here, June 1675.** Job Winslow's house east of the
  Kickemuit was plundered ~June 18–20; more houses burned June 19–23 (Hugh Cole's
  among them); on June 24 warriors killed nine settlers, the first English dead of
  the war — some ambushed near Swazey Corner, two more killed going for help. The
  colonial troops of Massachusetts Bay and Plymouth mustered at Rev. Myles's
  garrison house. The **Myles Garrison House site** is a stone marker with a bronze
  plaque at Barneyville Rd & Old Providence Rd. (americanhistorycentral.com
  "Swansea Attack, 1675"; historyofmassachusetts.org; hmdb.org m=299489;
  swanseahistoricalsociety.org.)
- **Abram's Rock** (Village Park). Legend: Abram, a Wampanoag who left his people
  to live among the settlers, hid from Philip's men in a boulder chamber still
  called "Abram's Bedroom"; captured, he was offered death or three leaps from the
  rock — survived two, died on the third. A puddingstone monolith; Wildcat Rock
  and Lion Rock nearby. (swanseamass.org/history/abramsrock.html;
  sowamsheritagearea.org.) The park's mapped viewpoint node is "wildcat rock".
- **Barneyville shipyard.** Jonathan Barney's yard (1770s), greatest under his son
  Mason — ~137 ships, an international reputation, closed 1860 with steam and the
  Civil War. Later a jewelry-making centre. (Wikipedia "Barneyville Historic
  District".)
- **Luther's Corner** = Swansea Center; a stagecoach stop, the town's economic
  centre in the first half of the 1800s. **Luther Store**, 160 Old Warren Rd:
  brick saltbox, built 1815 by John Brown Luther, a store till 1903, doubled as
  post office and library; Swansea Historical Society museum since 1941.
  (Wikipedia "Luther Store".)
- **Martin House Farm**, 22 Stoney Hill Rd (North Swansea): oldest part 1728 by John
  Martin; gambrel roof added c.1814; farmed by Martins until bequeathed in 1934 to
  the National Society of the Colonial Dames; NRHP 1978; farmhouse + two barns +
  stone-walled fields. (Wikipedia "Martin House and Farm"; nscdama.org.)
- **Swansea Friends Meeting House** (1701–02, oldest Quaker meetinghouse form in
  the state) is at 223 Prospect St, **Somerset** — Somerset was part of Swansea
  until 1790. Not a Swansea landmark today; a Somerset nod only if in frame.
- **The Stevens gifts.** Frank Shaw Stevens (d. 1898) and Elizabeth Case Stevens
  lived in the town's largest mansion (24 Main St, built 1855 for Julia Birch;
  mustard yellow; now Stevens Treatment Programs). Gifts to the town:
  - **Town Hall, 81 Main St — dedicated 1891**, Boston architect James Merrill
    Brown: random rubblestone with brownstone trim, a massive pyramidal slate
    roof, offset turret and clock tower; "one of the most unusual and
    architecturally eclectic town halls in New England".
  - **Swansea Free Public Library (Stevens Memorial Library), 69 Main St — 1900**,
    Henry Vaughan: Elizabethan, granite with red Potsdam sandstone; "one of the
    finest small-town buildings of its kind in Massachusetts".
  - **Christ Church, 57 Main St — 1900**, Henry Vaughan: Neo-Gothic, heavy granite
    walls, lancet windows, an entry tower with a rounded corner, battlements.
  (buildingsofnewengland.com — Swansea tag, "Christ Church Swansea // 1900";
  swanseahistoricalsociety.org; lizzieandrewborden.com Hatchet article.)
- **The summer colony.** The Colony Historic District at Gardner's Neck Rd &
  Mattapoisett Rd: seven Shingle / Colonial Revival summer cottages, 1896–1930 —
  the turn-of-the-century resort era; Ocean Grove's cottages have since become
  year-round homes. (Wikipedia "Colony Historic District"; homes.com Ocean Grove.)
- **Hurricanes.** The 1938 hurricane (Sept 21) and **Hurricane Carol (Aug 31,
  1954)** — Carol's surge came just after high tide; the Somerset and New Bedford
  areas took the worst of it in Massachusetts, Swansea among the affected shore
  towns. (NOAA AOML; mass.gov "worst hurricanes"; turnto10 gallery.)
- **Swansea Mall**, Route 6 & 118: opened 1975, ~90 stores; closed March 31, 2019;
  bought at auction by Anagnost (May 2019); gutted to a strip layout from 2021,
  the Apex wing demolished fall 2021; now "The Shoppes at Swansea", mostly vacant
  (a church in the old Macy's, self-storage in the Apex). (Wikipedia; wbsm.com;
  malls.fandom.) OSM still names the footprint "Swansea Mall".
- **Venus de Milo**, 75 GAR Hwy: opened 1959 (as a duckpin bowling alley by the
  Monsour family), grew into a 60,000 sq ft banquet hall for up to 2,000 — proms,
  weddings, politics; famous minestrone, baked stuffed lobster; Emeril Lagasse
  once cooked there. Family sold after 60 years; closed 2020, reopened under new
  owners. (pbn.com; Boston Globe 2020-09-16; turnto10; aol.)
- **Brayton Point** (Somerset, across the Lee River): coal plant 1963–2017; its two
  500-ft cooling towers (built 2009–, ~$620M) were imploded April 27, 2019 — the
  tallest cooling towers ever demolished; site now an offshore-wind port. (WBUR;
  CLF; UPI.)
- **Battleship Cove** (Fall River, under the Braga): opened Aug 14, 1965; USS
  Massachusetts "Big Mamie" (35,000 t, commissioned May 1942, Casablanca, Leyte
  Gulf, Okinawa), plus USS Joseph P. Kennedy Jr., USS Lionfish — all named
  footprints in OSM. (Wikipedia "Battleship Cove"; warfarehistorynetwork.)

## Kid-life (verified spots)

- **Swansea Town Beach, 560 Ocean Grove Ave** — public sandy beach on Mount Hope
  Bay, shallow (walk out hundreds of feet at low tide), lifeguards in summer, new
  bathhouse, playground; day/season passes. (swanseama.gov beach committee;
  thecrazytourist; yelp.)
- **Swansea Memorial Park** ("Veterans Memorial Park"), 347 GAR Hwy / 90 Milford Rd —
  big family playground, a mile of paved paths, Little League fields, tennis,
  fishing on the Mount Hope pond; links to the South Coast Bikeway. (swanseama.gov
  park commission.)
- **Village Park** — 194 protected acres behind Town Hall: trails, Abram's Rock,
  Wildcat Rock, a waterfall and dam at the end of the Lakeside Trail, "the Rusty
  Car", mountain-bike loops, a soccer field. (swanseama.gov conservation;
  singletracks; trailsandwalksri.)
- **The Ice Cream Barn** — homemade ice cream, outdoor seating, real cows on the
  property. (tripadvisor; theicecreambarn.com.) **Simcock Farm** (mapped
  `ice_cream` POI in north Swansea). **Somerset Creamery**, GAR Hwy at Gardner's
  Neck Rd (Somerset side of the line) — since 1937 / 2001 sources disagree; hand-
  made waffle cones. (somersetcreamery.com; tripadvisor.)
- **Joseph Case High School**, 70 School St — est. 1927, the **Cardinals**, South
  Coast Conference; football field at the school. **Case Junior High**, 195 Main
  St (grades 6–8). Elementaries: Gardner (10 Church St) and Hoyle (70 Community
  Ln) for pre-K–2; Brown and Luther (100 Pearse Rd) for 3–5. (Wikipedia; swanseaschools.org.)
- **Holiday in the Village** — early December on historic Main St: tree lighting
  at 5 pm with the Case High marching band, hay rides between Memorial Park and
  the Swansea Dam, Santa at the gazebo, touch-a-truck in the Case Jr High gym.
  (swanseama.gov; fallriverreporter.) **Memorial Day parade** through Ocean Grove
  and along Gardner's Neck Rd from St Francis of Assisi (530 Gardners Neck Rd) to
  the Veterans Memorial Green. (turnto10; patch.)
- **Cedar Cove Club**, 33 Cedar Cove Lane — seasonal beach/boating/tennis club.
  **Swansea Country Club** — par-72 public course. **Touisset Marsh Wildlife
  Refuge** (66 acres, seals and foxes) is on the Warren, RI side.

## Second pass (polish, same day)

- **Birch-Stevens Mansion, 24 Main St**: 1855, built for Julia Chase Birch while
  James Birch ran a stagecoach business in Gold Rush California; Birch drowned in
  the *Central America* wreck of 1857; Julia married his partner Frank Shaw
  Stevens in 1858. "Grand Italianate: low hipped roof with belvedere, broad
  bracketed eaves, paired arched windows, expansive wrap-around porch."
  Elizabeth Stevens's will (1930) made it a home for boys — the Frank S. Stevens
  Home opened 1939, today Stevens Treatment Programs. (buildingsofnewengland.com
  Frank S. Stevens tag; swanseahistoricalsociety.org; stevensprograms.org.)
- **First Baptist Church in Swansea, 21 Baptist St**: 1848, vernacular Greek
  Revival — pedimented facade, pilasters dividing the bays, a square belfry;
  built under Rev. J. J. Thatcher (pastor 1846–54); the congregation's fifth
  building; NRHP Feb 16, 1990. (Wikipedia; Buildings of New England 2026-04-17.)
- **The Swansea Dam**: Lewin Brook "meanders into Swansea Dam" (kayaking video
  title "Swansea Dam, Lewin Brook"); Village Park's Lakeside Trail ends at "a
  dam and waterfall", trailhead behind the library (Trails & Walks RI); mindat
  also lists a separate "Lewin Brook Pond Upper Dam" downstream. The in-game dam
  sits at the Village Park lake's south tip below Main Street.
- **Abram's Rock**: no public coordinates found (the GC3B192 earthcache page
  holds them); Wildcat Rock is OSM's only mapped viewpoint in the park.
- **Neighbour dates** (for borderLore): Rehoboth inc. 1645, Dighton 1712,
  Somerset 1790 (from Swansea), Seekonk 1812 (from Rehoboth), Fall River city
  1854, Bristol RI Oct 28 1681, Barrington RI 1717 (re-established 1770),
  Warren RI 1747, East Providence 1862.

## Building heights (why Swansea carries `overtureHeightScale: 1.3`)

Devin's first look at the live town: "so many of the homes aren't the right
height, a lot are 1 story instead of 2". The numbers agreed. With the overlay's
ridge thresholds as calibrated on North Shore knowns (5.2 m → 1, 7.2 m → 1.5,
above → 2+), Swansea's 27,562 Overture heights split **69% / 27% / 5%**; every
other town splits 35–46% / 33–37% / 21–28%. Checks made before touching it:

- **Not release drift.** Amesbury re-fetched on the 2026-08-19 release: 11,267
  matched footprints, every height byte-identical to the 2026-06-17 data.
- **Swansea's own OSM floor tags** (106 buildings with both a tag and an ML
  height): tagged two-storey homes read a median 5.0 m (p25 4.3, p75 5.7) —
  under the one-storey cutoff. Tagged one-storey read 4.9 m. The ML barely
  separates the two here; the whole distribution is compressed (p50 4.6 m, p90
  6.3 m vs 5.4–5.8 / 8.7–9.0 up north).
- A multiplier fits the compression; an offset does not (offsets flood the 1.5
  band). Candidate scales against the North Shore mix: 1.25 → 35/47/18,
  **1.3 → 28/50/22**, 1.35 → 25/49/26. 1.3 chosen: it restores a two-storey
  share in the North Shore range while keeping a real ranch share (Swansea is
  a cape-and-ranch suburb too). The 1.5-storey lean that remains is the source
  data's, not a threshold choice.

If a street still reads low on the ground, raise the scale in `town.json` and
re-bake — it is one number, and explicit OSM floor tags are never scaled.

**Superseded the same evening by the assessor overlay.** Devin: "a lot of houses
aren't the right height" (still) and "the Swansea Mall is not brick". Both are
now answered from the town's own records: MassGIS Level 3 parcels (M292, plus
Somerset 273, Rehoboth 247, Dighton 76, Seekonk 265 for the frame) carry
`STORIES` and `STYLE` per lot — Swansea has 1,636 Ranch / 1,291 Colonial /
1,096 Cape Cod / 577 Raised Ranch lots — so the largest house on each lot takes
its assessed storeys (Swansea houses: 42% one / 25% cape / 28% two-plus, per
house rather than per guess), garages drop to one, and the DOR use code
reclassifies big untagged footprints: 419 commercial, 68 industrial, 66 civic,
9 churches. That is what turns the Target/Marshalls plaza (a 17,358 m²
"house" at three storeys) into a one-storey commercial box, the police station
and the Y into civic, and lets `masonryMix: 0.7` paint the Route 6 strip in
beige block instead of brick. The Overture scale stays for lots the assessor
has nothing on (Rhode Island).

Materials researched: the Shoppes at Swansea (the 2021 gutting made it an
exterior-entrance strip — `buildStrip`), Walmart (its tan-and-blue livery),
the Venus (white, "grand white columns at its entrance" — pbn.com), Case High
(1927 brick-and-granite Colonial Revival core per the MHC inventory SWN.46;
`buildModernSchool` is brick). The elementaries are modelled brick on the
strength of every 1950s–60s Massachusetts school being brick — unverified.

## Unverified / do not model

- Christ Church's exact footprint: OSM has no tagged church on Main St; the
  address anchor (57 Main) is used for the landmark, no hero.
- Whether the Town Hall is still the clock-tower building in use (it is listed as
  81 Main St on the town site; assumed yes).
- Swansea Dam location: taken as the Lewin Brook Pond outlet by the village
  (Holiday-in-the-Village hay rides run "from Memorial Park to the Swansea Dam";
  Village Park's trail ends at "a dam and waterfall"). Marked as a pond landmark,
  not a dam hero.
